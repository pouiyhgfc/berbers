De dialogen

Een heel gesprek volgen.

book p.169–174

Grammar: §18.2

Translation pending — this lesson has not been translated into English yet.

Alles wat je geleerd hebt, in echte gesprekken. Deze dialogen komen uit hoofdstuk 18 van het boek — dit is hoe mensen werkelijk met elkaar praten.

Waar je op kunt letten

Terwijl je meeleest, herken je de bouwstenen uit de hele cursus terug: de begroetingen van les 01, het partikel qa uit les 09, de vraagwoorden van les 21, de ontkenning met waa … ca uit les 22. Een echt gesprek gebruikt alles tegelijk, en juist daarom is dit de beste oefening.

Let ook op de gespreksvulling die in geen enkele grammatica staat maar overal opduikt: iwa "nou…", waxxa "oké", wah "ja", en d Wenni netta — "dat is prima", het vaste afsluitertje voordat mensen afscheid nemen.

Zelf oefenen

Lees eerst alleen de Tarifit-kolom en kijk hoe ver je komt. Dek daarna de Tarifit af en probeer vanuit het Nederlands terug te bouwen. De dialogen zijn kort genoeg om uit je hoofd te leren — en dat is precies wat je nodig hebt om je eerste echte gesprek te voeren.

familya marra mliḥ?

Is your family doing well?

marra mliḥ, l-hamdu li-lah, barek llahu fik

They are all fine, praise the Lord, thank you.

min ttɛawaded?

What are you up to? (lit. ‘what are you telling’)

iwa, qa ttwarid...

Well, you see...

waxxa, ad uyuaɣ řexxu, ɣari ca n ccɣeṛ

Okay, I’m leaving now, I have to do something.

waxxa, d Wenni netta, b-eslama

Allright, that’s fine, bye bye.

d Wenni netta, b-eslama

That’s fine, bye bye.

tessawared tmazixt?

mliḥ, mani teɣrid tmazixt?

I learned it at University.

mliḥ, mamec das-qqaan?

Good, what is your name? (lit. ‘what do they call you’)

mliḥ, mamec dam-qqaan?

Good, what is your name? (lit. ‘what do they call you’)

(necc) qqaan-ayi Patrik (Mariya)

(Me), my name is Patrick (Maria). (lit. ‘they call me Patrick’)

waxxa, necc qqaan-ayi Mimun (Fatima)

Okay, I’m leaving, we will see each other, God willing.

in-caɛ-ellah ad yewwet ɛaweḏ

It will rain again, God willing.

mařa yexs aṛṛebbi in-caɛ-ellah

God willing.

in-caɛ-ellah

God willing.

waxxa, mani ɣa nraḥ?

min texsed a tecced?

b-ism-illah

tuɣa itazzer ɛini. iwa, a ten-yaaḥem siḏi aabbi.

Het sprookje

Een doorlopend verhaal lezen.

book p.134–141

Grammar: §18.1

Translation pending — this lesson has not been translated into English yet.

Een doorlopend verhaal: een jeugdherinnering over hoe grootmoeder 's avonds sprookjes vertelde. Dit is echt, geschreven Tarifit — geen lesvoorbeelden meer, maar literatuur.

De openingszin ontleed

Het verhaal begint zo:

Am neccin am wattas n yewdan mammec i ɛeqreɣ — "ik, zoals veel mensen, zoals ik het me herinner…"

Stuk voor stuk herken je wat je geleerd hebt:

  • am — "zoals", het voorzetsel uit les 24
  • neccin — "wij/ik", vrij voornaamwoord (les 03)
  • wattas — "veel" in de verbonden staat na am (les 13)
  • n yewdan — "van mensen", bezit met n (les 14)
  • mammec — "hoe", vraagwoord dat hier als voegwoord dienstdoet (les 21)
  • i — de relatiefmarker (les 30)
  • ɛeqreɣ — "ik herinner mij", vervoegd op -eɣ (les 06)

Zeven lessen in één regel van acht woorden. Zo dicht is natuurlijk Tarifit.

Nog een patroon om op te letten

In tuɣa xminni i ɣa raheɣ ad ttseɣ deggwxxam — "wanneer ik in de kamer ging slapen" — zie je drie dingen tegelijk: tuɣa zet het geheel in het verleden (les 09), na het voegwoord xminni wordt ad netjes ɣa (les 26), en deggwxxam is een samengesmolten voorzetsel plus naamwoord (les 35).

Lees het als een verhaal

probeer niet elk woord te ontleden. Lees een alinea door, pak de draad, en kijk pas daarna naar de details. Verhalen leren je taal op een andere manier dan tabellen dat doen.

Am neccin am wattas n yewdan mammec i ɛeqreɣ

I, like many people, the way I remember,

tuɣa xminni i ɣa raheɣ ad ttseɣ deggwxxam

when I would go sleeping in the room

jaa yayetma d yemma,

between my brothers and my mother,

yemma tuɣa yaa a tessexsi ṯaftic n fqendif

my mother would just put out the wick of the oil lamp

niɣ a tsud x teftic n tsumeet a t-tessexsi,

or blow on the wick of the candle in order to put it out,

uca a tessenta a daneɣ-d-ṯini ijjen tḥajit zi tinni yessaggwaden attas

and then she would begin to tell us one of the very scary stories,

ḥima a nesqaa, niɣ ḥima a daneɣ-yawi yides ḏeɣya.

so that we would keep quiet, or so that we would fall asleep immediately (lit. so that sleep would carry us away immediately).

Mammec tuɣa kidney ttnus ḥenna ca n twafatin

Like when my grandmother would sleep at our place some times,

uřa d nettaṯ tuɣa teqqar-aneɣ-d tiḥuja zi tinni yessaggwaden attas.

she would also tell us very scary stories.

Amecnaw tḥajit n wemziw niɣ amecnaw tḥajit-enni n tenni yeccin mmi-s

Like the story of the ogre, or like the story of the woman that ate her son,

niɣ n tenni i tuɣa yetraḥen s ǧiret ɣaa imedran

or of the woman that used to go to the cemetery at night

tfetter dinni seysu s ufus umettin.

and roll couscous there with the hand of a dead person.

ḥenna tuɣa tessen ijjen wattas n tḥuza

My grandmother knew a lot of stories

uca tuɣa xminni i daneɣ-d-teqqar ca n tḥajit

then, when she would tell us a story

a teqqaa a teqqaa a daneɣ-d-tɛawed tḥajit-enni

she would tell and tell and tell us the story,

ar daneɣ-tesra marra neccuxrut,

until she heard us all snoring.

xenni i ɣa tessen ḥenna belli yiwy-aneɣ yides

when my grandmother would know that we had fallen asleep

uca a tesqaa ḥima uřa d nettaṯ a tettes.

she would stop speaking so that she could sleep too.

maca ca n twaratin tesriɣ-as i ḥenna ttraɣa-ayi-d

But sometimes I heard my grandmother call me:

‘A ɛri inu, a ɛri inu, ma tettsed niɣ ɛad waa tettised?’

‘Ali, my Ali, are you asleep, or are you not yet asleep?’

A das-iniɣ necc: ‘A ḥenna, necc ɛad waa ttiseɣ ca.'

I would answer: ‘Grandmother, I’m not yet asleep.’

Uca a tsiɣi ḥenna a dayi-d-teqqar tḥajit-enni.

Then my grandmother would continue telling me the story.

uca tuɣa ḥenna kuř ṯwaṛa a tesqaa,

Then my grandmother would time and again stop speaking,

‘A ɛri inu, a ɛri inu, ma tettsed niɣ ɛad? Waa tettised ca?’

She would ask me time and again,

txezzar-ayi ma yiwy-ayi yides niɣ ɛad.

ruḥ xac, a xac nraḥ waa nteggwed! ḥazit-ek!

'We will go through it without fear. ’

tuɣa waa-ssi seḇɛa uřa ṯřaṯa,

necc muhimm ssneɣ ṯřaṯa, ad iniɣ ṯřaṯa.

netta tuɣa-t dinni, tuɣa yetɛesses, tuɣa yennuffaa yesḥessa ɣaasent.

Ixedb-it, yiwy-it, yiwy-az-d ṯrist,

ṯrist uhuri amen tekmer.

an entire ram’s fleece.

Teǧm-it tqaadc-it, hay hay, ca iruḥ diha, ca tzery-it,

waa das-t-teggi ca, waa das-teggi hu uqubbu.

She did not make it for him, she did not make a cloak for him,

Waa das-teggi ra d ṛqebb.

She did not even make a hood for him.

lɛawed i tenneɣni.

He did the same to the other.

Yiwi tenni das-yennan:

He married the one that had said to him:

‘a das-ggeɣ seksu s yijjen ṯiḏaatṯ’.

‘I will make you couscous from a single ear of grain’

netta yiwy-as-d řmuḏḏ, řmuḏḏ n yiaden.

He married her and brought her a mud, a mud of wheat.

Thaay-iten, tssiff-iten, ca iruḥ t ṯanexxač,

She ground it, she sieved it, some of it became bran,

cway iruḥ d iwzan, cwayt t ṯiwzaṭin,

some of it became crushed grains, some finely crushed grains,

cway d aan t taneɣda.

some flour like powder.

Waa das-teggi fa t tratac.

She did not even make for him any couscous rolling.

Taneggarut yiwy-it, tenni das-yennan:

He married the last one, the one that had said to him:

‘a das-d-jjeɣ mmi-s ɣaas ṯiyuqiṯ n wuaɣ di tenyiat.’

‘I will give you a son with a golden pearl on his forehead.’

Yiwy-it yesbaa, ttak ttak, ɣaas ddiseṭ, yetɛayan.

He married her, he waited patiently, ttak ttak, she became pregnant, he waited,

Yetɛayan, nhar-a ṯiwecca, nhar-a ṯiwecca,

he waited, day after day, day after day.

yiwed-d xas tsɛe chua, tzeyyed, turu-d,

She reached her nine months (lit. nine months reached her), and she gave birth,

turu-d mmi-s ɣaas ṯiyuqiṯ n wuaɣ di tenyiat.

she gave birth to a son with a golden pearl on his forehead.

Tekkaa tacniwin nnes ɣiant zzayes nnant-as:

The other wives became jealous and said:

- Xyaa taniti tejj-d mmi-s ɣaas ṯiyuqiṯ n wuaɣ di tenyiat,

- Okay, she has given birth to a son with a golden pearl on her forehead,

neccin min nenna waa neggi ca, taniti a ṯiři tefder xaneɣ?!

we, we did not do what we had promised, and she, she should be better than us?!

Tekkaa-d tekk-d senni ijjen tmesɛect,

So a beggar woman passed by,

neccin neqqar-as ṯameṣɛecṯ, ttettaa.

we call it a beggar women, she begged.

nettaṯ meskina mix d-tejja aḥenjia-nni, tesxef safi.

The poor one had fully fainted when she had given birth to the boy.

Wsint-as tehru uca sfesyent-as ddhen,

They gave it to her, she drank, they melted butter for her,

teggent ddegg-nni, safi teshed, ttekk adu tmuat.

They did that sort of thing, then she fainted, she fainted (lit. she passed under the ground)

Ksint-as aḥenjia-nni nnant-as, tmeɛɛec, tenn-as:

They took the boy away from her and said to her, she was begging, she said to her:

- A xači waa yaaney da ca, yaaney illa icten turn,

- Aunty, we don’t have anything here, we only have someone who has given birth,

tendaa mmi-s tugi a t-tessyem,

and thrown away her son, she refuses to raise him.

mařa texsed a dam-t-newc a t-tesseymed.

If you want, we shall give him to you so that you can raise him.

Tenn-as: - wah a yessi ewcentt-ayi-t.

She said: - Okay, my daughters, give him to me.

Teksi-t nettaṯ.

She took him with her.

uca wcint-as, qessent-as tirettett,

So they gave him to her, they cut off his little finger,

ggint-as-t deg qemmum i yemma-s.

and put it in the mouth of his mother.

Ggint-as-t deg qemmum, tuyua,

They put it in the mouth (of his mother) and she went away,

wcint-as aseymi, sseḥnunndent-t di tsunnad, di tkettanin.

they gave her the baby, they wrapped him in swaddling clothes, in cloths.

Tsseḥnunnd-it di tsunnad nnes teksi-t.

She wrapped him in swaddling clothes and she took him with her.

Teksi-t tmesɛect-nni, safi.

The beggar woman took him away, that’s it.

kkaa ggint-as tirettett-nni deg qemmum umi tuyua tmesɛect-nni,

So they put the little finger in her mouth when the beggar woman had gone away,

mbeɛd umi t-twaran ggua a tfaq, sekkaant tɣuyyit;

and then, when they saw that she was going to wake up they started to cry:

- Aaaaa qa ta tecca mmi-s,

Izran: Riffijnse liederen

Coupletten lezen en herkennen.

book p.164–168

Grammar: §18.1

Translation pending — this lesson has not been translated into English yet.

Izran zijn korte Riffijnse liederen — twee regels, gezongen bij bruiloften, tijdens het werk, of gewoon om je hart te luchten. Ze zijn eeuwenoud en tegelijk springlevend.

Hoe een izri werkt

Elk couplet bestaat uit twee regels die op elkaar rijmen of elkaar spiegelen. De taal is compact en beeldend: één regel schetst een beeld uit de natuur, de volgende slaat dat om naar een gevoel. Zo staat er in kkiɣ sadu wuatu ḥesbeɣ ṯifray nnes — "ik liep onder de vijgenboom door en telde zijn bladeren" — een beeld dat pas in de volgende regel zijn betekenis krijgt.

De opening

Veel izran beginnen met een aanroeping van God, precies zoals verhalen dat doen: bismillah a nebda, bismillah a nenteq — "in Gods naam zullen wij beginnen, in Gods naam zullen wij spreken".

Terugkerende thema's

Liefde en gemis voeren de boventoon — vaak over iemand die naar Europa vertrok. mařa truḥed Aliman qqbbi waa das-isemmeh — "als je naar Duitsland gaat, moge de Heer je niet vergeven" — is geen vloek maar liefdesverdriet: emigratie is in het Rif een vertrouwd verdriet.

Poëzie speelt met de regels

in liederen wordt de woordvolgorde vrijer en verdwijnen woordjes die in gewoon proza verplicht zijn. Verbaas je niet als een regel de grammatica uit deze cursus oprekt; dat hoort erbij.

bismillah a nebda, bismillah a nenteq

In God’s name we will begin, in God’s name we will speak,

bismillah a nekkes zeggw ur nneɣ axeyyeq

In God’s name we will take away our sadnecc.

bismillah a nebda ɛad waa nebdi zi ca

In God’s name we will begin, we still haven’t begun.

a nexzaa ma ntawy-it-d niɣ waa t-id-ntiwi ca

We shall see whether we are good at it or whether we are not.

ad truɣ ad truɣ wi ɣa rqiɣ a t-ssruɣ

I will cry and cry, whoever I encounter I will make him cry.

mami leɛziz inu i ma ɛad a t-ttuɣ?

My sweetheart will I be able to forget him?

kkiɣ sadu wuatu ḥesbeɣ ṯifray nnes

I passed under the fig tree and counted its leaves

mmerqiɣ ak llif min iru wur nnes

I met my sweetheart, his heart wept a lot.

mařa truḥed Aliman qqbbi waa das-isemmeh

If you go to Germany may the Lord not forgive you

tejjid fesqef inu deggw brid iseyyeh

You left my mind spilled all over the road.

mařa truḥed Aliman gg-ayi di rbar nnec

If you go to Germany, keep me in your thoughts

awi-d arriɣalu ɛemmaa-d řejbub nnec

Bring me a present, fill your pockets.

a řeḇḥaa a řeḇḥaa ayaṛṛabu sennej

O sea, o sea with a boat on it,

wi ɣa iraḥen Aliman a leɛziz a cem-yejj?

Who can go to Germany and leave you behind?

a itran ujenna sennej i ṯaddaaṯ trun

O stars in the sky, crying above the house,

ruḥ siwdem-as ssram i ten dayi-yettun

Go and send my regards to the girl that forgot me.

iḍes walu walu a ttebriɣ s kukayin

I can’t get no sleep. I’m addicted to cocaine

min xafi icedden tinni n ccayatin?

What got me hooked on the works of the devil?

aya ṛalla yemma, a yemma inu qettiɣ

O my dear mother, I am losing weight.

iwḏan ssawaren di ddat inu tesriɣ

People talk and in my body I hear it.

siḏi ɛri n nnadua a rmaasa n teḥramin

Sidi Ali of Nador, o harbour of girls,

ad isebbaa arebbi ur inu meskin

May God provide my poor heart with patience.

yexs-icem wur inu yexs-icem zi ṯemẓi

My heart loves you, it loves you since childhood

mayemmi dayi-tejjid, tejjid-ayi da wehd-i?

Why have you left me, left me here alone?

aya muřay nneɣ a uma inu sseɛḏ nnec

O our groom, my beloved brother, how lucky you are,

wellah illa tiwyed tenni yexs wur nnec

I swear that you have married the one whom your heart desires.

aya muřay nneɣ a zzin d arifi

O our groom, o Riffian beauty,

zzin am wanita i mani ɣa yiri?

A beauty like this, could there be anywhere else?

awi-d aǧun-nni tuɣa nniɣ nehřa

Give me that tambourine which I did not want to have,

amcum-a n wur inu yugi a dayi-yehna

This damned heart of mine is restless.

ṛalla ṯasřiṯ nneɣ mebruk a wecma

Our bride, congratulations o sister,

neccin ntedɛa akidem s řehna

We pray that you find peace.

ṛalla ṯasřiṯ nneɣ taqedduḥt n danun

Our bride, o can of Danone (a yoghurt mark)

aabbi yewc-am zzin waa dam-t-yewci s řqanun

God has given you beauty in abundance.

a talimaniyyet, ma ammu i yexs wur nnem?

O German woman, is this your heart’s desire?

sellm-as a d-yaaggweh, char inu aan nnem.

Allow him to go home, one month he is mine, the year round he is yours.

mayen yuɣin ur inu, ibedd waa ixeddem.

What happened to my heart, it stands still and doesn’t work

yenna-ayi wedbib: d lḥubb i dayem.

The doctor told me: It is that you are in love.

tus-ed tenna-ayi: llif nnem isekkaa.

She came to me and said: Your sweetheart always gets drunk

mařa netta yescaa, necc ad sweɣ kṯaa

if he gets drunk, I will drink (even) more.

waa ɣari bu řeɛḏu zeggw ɣarrabu n Mřič

I have no enemy except for the Melilla boat

yiwy-ayi mami ynu, yejj-ayi t tayujic

It took away my love, and left me (like) an orphan.
